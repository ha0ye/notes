# Marston All-Staff
2021-07-13

presentation on: 2021-07-20

key concepts:
* make it easy for patrons to do the thing you want
* be proactive about creating inclusive pathways
  - who is not represented in data/feedback?
  - not everyone will tell you what they need


building an inclusive environment

my background
* evidence-based pedagogy
* ally skills teaching

premise (collective agreement that)
* libraries should be inclusive
* systems that we participate in are not equitable
  - through no fault of our own
  - but if we can see it and have the power to fix it, then there is responsibility to do so

fair != equitable
* e.g. not having ramps for disabled patrons is "fair", but not equitable (also not legal)
* changes to enable everyone to participate equally may require extra work and extra effort
  - that's ok, because being inclusive is more important than penny-pinching

how do we identify problems
* expecting members of underserved groups to raise issues is unfair
  - tokenization
  - retaliation
* e.g. when I teach programming, learners can be a bit reluctant to ask questions or for me to repeat something - if that is a challenge in a learning environment where I am supposed to be teaching, how do you expect people to call out every microaggression?

studies on minority-serving effort

* ways to be proactive
  - identify whose needs are not being met
  - multiple pathways to do the thing (whether it's finding a resource, asking questions, providing feedback, filling out a survey, etc.)

* facilitating equitable meetings
  - assign and rotate responsibilities (don't ask for volunteers)
  - recognize auto-transcription isn't perfect
  - shared notes (may require a facilitator)

strategies for responding to incidents
* what's funny about X (pretend to be naive)
  - can work if you know offender is unintentional, if you want a teaching moment
* "stop" / "we don't do that here"
  - stop the behavior immediately (treat it as any other transgression - e.g. smoking in the library)
* NO humor
  - harm isn't a funny subject
  - very very common to make fun of e.g. physical appearance, intelligence, attractiveness, etc. (you end up harming others)

when someone raises an issue
* it can mean that a problem has already affected many people

apologizing
* your feelings of guilt/shame are valid! (but they aren't relevant to an apology in the moment)
* apologize for the action
* commit to doing better - communicate this how you want
