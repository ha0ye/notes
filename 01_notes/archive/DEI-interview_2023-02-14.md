Sr. Director of DEI Phone Interview Questions

2023-02-14
LaKeshia Darden

1.     What inspired you to apply for this position? [Melody]

I was excited when I saw this position
From reading the description, I would be focused on DEI in the libraries
in my past positions, DEI was not a major part of my duties
many hats as a reference librarian
cultural competency has been a part
this has been my passion, and an excitign direction for me.

2.     What do you see as the current and most challenging aspects for libraries in an increasingly diverse academic community? What steps have you taken or plan to take to meet such challenges? [Hao]

providing respectful spaces for all differing viewpoints
in the process of that, academic libraries have to acknowledge our history of exclusion of certain groups - how we have evolved from that
access to all types of people and all types of information
* censorship - one way is to diversify collection development

specific example, predominantly white baptist university
libguides were of heavily southern white baptist tradition
compare to libguides of faith-based HBCU and public universities
there was a bias in our slant and our collection
comparison to sister institution, and make sure to provide different viewpoints

respectful - use "respectful" instead of safe - who is it safe for? spaces should be respectful of all different view points

make libraries more welcoming for student body
use IPEDS data - feature different publications from those host countries
at Campbell, we have a large population of Indian students, large military population - pull in military periodicals and have them on display for who we were serving.

3.     Please describe your policy development and stakeholder engagement experience, particularly any experience coordinating a collaborative project. [Haven]

policy - will be small scale - libqual surveys - several responses of patrons of not feeling welcome in the library, comments that staff were not being welcoming (e.g. could not sleep in the library, had to be studying) - cultural awareness committee within libraries - I formed the committee, made sure to have someone from eevery department on the committee; go through policies for the libraries
* found out that no one was following the policies, because they weren't working - laptop policy was checkout for 4 hours, renew for 8 hours, late fee of $200 - students who checked them out did not have their own computer - GAs were waiving the fees for students
* different things like that - go through policies and change them if they weren't working for the people they were designed to serve
* patrons will wonder why policies are enforced by this librarian but not this other librarian

4.     Describe your experiences in evaluating and assessing diversity, equity, and inclusion initiatives and their outcomes. [Ginessa]

started staff common reads? did pre- and post- reading surveys
would ask questions related to reading topics (e.g. culturally aware)

form groups on MS Teams to keep thread, keep discussion going

survey questions, what was positive, what was negative
exit survey - what did they learn, what they would use in their future

5.     Please tell us about your experience in teaching and training, including in developing training materials related to DEI. [Plato] 

I started as a high school english teacher, 8 years
I transitioned into librarianship
for the first few years, i was a reference librarian, and then co-teaching with a children's librarian - pushed me to go back to get my EdD 
option to teaching or stay in librarianship
that's when I started focusing on DEI - went back to undergraduate school (HBCU) - I've always been in predominantly white institutions, bringing DEI into my teaching
what could I do to incorporate librarianship in teaching - use black children's literature in teaching for white librarian professionals

first one I did was dissertation study - I had to figure out which books to use, use coretta scott king award winners - want to give different experiences - black adult, children, athlete experiences

I listed several trainings on my CV for Campbell - Dean was supportive during my dissertation

one thing about my experience - DEI training was not from an outside perspective, I was doing it with my coworkers, different emotional load to navigate

6.     Tell us your experience discussing or presenting on sensitive topics to varied audiences, and further, how would you approach making diversity relevant or valued to individuals or groups who do not see its value? [Tabby]

With pre-service teachers. When I am discussing DEI and culturally-responsive teaching in the classroom. I do use with them a lot of different books. I use books as a backdrop to spearhead discussion, e.g. white privilege. Might use a children's book to lead the discussion

One book that I was use with students and with staff, "crossover" Kwami Alexander, middle-grade level written in verse; dad is a retired basketball star, 2 sons who are twins, mother is a vice-principal at the school they attend
* readers were not expecting this type of family
* Josh was one of the boys and loves words; unexpected
* love letter from author, did not have his personal experience
getting my students to see their preconceived notion of what a black family is; there are all different types of experiences

"brave face" is a memoir by Shawn Hutchens; tries to come out to his family. I chose this at Campbell. I went to counseling department - asked what were issues that our students were dealing with - many were struggling with sexuality. What could we use to talk about this with faculty and staff, to not judge or condemn.
At first, was not well-received. "What is the goal?" It is an issue that our students are struggling with, may be issues that students are not comfortable engaging with family or classmates about

I use books or narrative theory to get into difficult topics, lead to more discussions, what are we going to do about it.

follow-through on "brave face" on Teams followup chat
* some people did come into contact with students later on, we would give feedback or support to folks on listen
* how to support students and listen to students even if it is something they personally they disagree with; had to learn how to help my colleagues navigate

7.     The Senior Director of DEI may sometimes need to be bold and reconcile with the discomfort of being an advocate for DEI values that library deans and/or chairs may not agree with. Please provide an example of a time when you had to champion a more just outcome or decision. [Melody]

During COVID, before George Floyd. I was trying to put together cultural awareness timeline of events for each semester. Dean felt that because there was so much going on with COVID, scale back events.
I had some common reads and discussions, webinars and workshops.
pushback from Dean, I felt there were issues on campus that need to be addressed; need to be proactive and not reactive
racial profiling that was happening on campus; Dean wanted to focus more on students in the community through COVID; felt that it would stress staff out

a few weeks later, George Floyd incident happened; I took a personal mental day - go forward with calendar; racism is still there 
will always be with us, and needs to be addressed consistently

have to look at institution, what do we need in our own backyard, what do we need to be addressed

cultural awareness space and workshops need to exist and be consistent

not sure if we would have been able to do the cultural awareness issues
some leadership will always feel other things are more important
DEI is something I will always advocate for

8.     What is one idea you are excited about pursuing in increasing or enhancing diversity, equity, inclusion? [Hao]

I would like to develop this whole using children's books as diversity training. I have witnessed different consulting groups - not as threatening - have used other books like "white fragility" or "how to be an antiracist" - have a knee jerk reaction. Adults tend to give more grace to young protagonists.
adults remember when they were young and learning new things
in my observations, people are more critical of young adults and children
I would like to use more YA and children literature
would like to delve more into it
come up with ways of assessment, e.g. surveys and followups and chats and discussion group

may be more?

one-shot DEI, workshop here, webinar here, how do you followup? are they implementing these things?

narrative theory is what I use in my dissertation; change in thinking about the topic that was discussed in the text

I have seen that change in thinking happen with children's literature
