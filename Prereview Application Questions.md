Equity, diversity, inclusion, and accessibility (EDIA) all have somewhat different individual meanings, but broadly speaking, I interpret EDIA as pointing in the direction of valuing people as individuals and welcoming their input and participation in communities and workplaces.

#### Understanding Openness: What does “open” mean to you in the context of scholarly communication? Feel free to include values, practices, or challenges that shape your perspective.

Maybe it is because of my background as a computational ecologist, but to me "open" means that all steps and intermediate products that occur in the process of conducting research are shared openly (to the extent that it is reasonable).

I distinctly remember a moment towards the end of my PhD, when I tried to reproduce the results of a published paper. After exchanging some emails with the authors, they still would not share their code, but only linked the software library that they used. I spent several frustrating months trying to reproduce their analysis without getting anywhere. Some years later I talked with a colleague who also unsuccessfully tried to reproduce their results! Although it validated my experience, I still do not know to what extent the results of that paper might have been problematic or depended on key methodological details that were not shared.

I don't think my own experience is unique; and I expect that many decisions (in the operationalization of a research question, selection of samples and instrumentation, implementation, and analysis & interpretation of results) are not thoroughly and transparently reported. The extent to which this impacts science is not well-known, but I think some of the obvious negative impacts are in the rigor of research results (e.g. irreproducibility due to vagueness or errors), and in the equity of credit (e.g. good scholarship in citations and acknowledgement of contributors). 

(my work is trying to improve openness to address both axes)





#### Fostering Belonging: Can you share an example of when you helped foster (or witnessed the creation of) a culture of belonging in a community or workplace? What was your role?

In 2023, I was the lead organizer of a special session, "Designing the future of open data: A discussion with the Data Paper editors of 'Ecology'", at the annual meeting of the Ecological Society of America.

As one of a team of 4 data paper editors at 'Ecology', our goal was to engage with our audience of ecological researchers to gather perspectives on publishing ecological data in the journal. Some of our key interests were in identifying ways of updating processes to:
* improve submission and review processes
* increase submissions, and their diversity and quality
* align practices with open science movements (e.g. data sharing broadly, and other forms of open science)

In designing the sessions as an unconference-style guided discussion, our concerns about belongingness had to do with promoting interaction in a short amount of time, between random strangers, straddling a mixture of career stages, and overcoming  reluctances in sharing personal opinions and perspectives.

We settled on several key design aspects of the session:
1. structuring the room layout to promote small groups where individuals would have more opportunity to speak up
2. using an icebreaker activity to normalize sharing of something personal, and demonstrating how the session would proceed
(We instructed everyone to write **one emoji you would use to describe your subfield, study organism, or study biome (OR if none exists, what emoji would you add to the existing standard)** on a post-it, and to attach the post-it to our sign-in board)
3. rotating groups across physical stations and different prompts,
