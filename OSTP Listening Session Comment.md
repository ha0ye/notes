Hi, my name is Hao Ye. For the last 3 years, I have been the Reproducibility Librarian at the University of Florida, providing instruction and support for open science.

I have 3 main comments about the needs of institutions in regard to Open Science.

First, it is not enough to merely create new policy; practical guidance is needed. With the rollout of the 2023 NIH Data Management and Sharing Policy, many were left confused about how to write a plan or choose a repository. In making the policy general to accommodate diverse researcher needs, confusion abounded. I led a working group of data librarians to create guidance materials to be shared widely, but it would have been more effective to be in a working partnership with NIH.

Second, I'm sure comments in other sessions have remarked on incentives. I would like to emphasize the dangers of relying on easily quantifiable metrics over the need to support many kinds of research practices that do not fit into neat registered-report "boxes".

Third, the flipside of incentives is enforcement. So far, there appears to be very little effective investigation into and consequences for research misconduct, including harassment and abuse. Making open science commonplace means that we have to uphold community norms about "what" effective and rigorous research means. I would welcome novel approaches that do not rely solely on researcher self-governance and also do not involve a "research police".
